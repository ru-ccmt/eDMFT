1. Exact symmetry adjoint of normalization
=========================================
Definitions: P_k is the raw band-by-correlated-orbital projector; w_k are
constant normalized k weights. Sraw=sum_k w_k P_k^dagger P_k is the orbital
overlap before symmetry. X=S^(-1/2) and U_k=P_k X are the normalization and
normalized projector. Orbital indices and separate correlated blocks are
implicit. Herm(A)=(A+A^dagger)/2.

The implemented forward map is

  S = F[Sraw],   F = Herm o E o A,
  A[Sraw] = (1/|G|) sum_g R_g^dagger Sraw R_g.

R_g is the orbital representation of a crystal operation. E averages the
equivalent correlated blocks in the established common local basis. The
derivative of X is the Frechet derivative of the inverse square root:

  X^(-1) dX + dX X^(-1) = -X dS X.

Let C_k be the orbital-by-band functional gradient defined by
dGamma_P=2 Re sum_k Tr(C_k dU_k), with frequency/k/spin weights included in C.
Define J=Herm sum_k C_k P_k. If L_X[dS]=dX, then

  Q=L_X[J],
  dGamma_norm = 2 Re Tr(Q dS)
              = 2 Re Tr(F^*[Q] dSraw).

The Frechet map is self-adjoint on Hermitian matrices. However, its ordering
relative to the symmetry map must not be changed without a commutation
proof. The correct reverse chain is

  Qeff = F^*[Q] = A^* E Herm[Q],
  A^*[Q] = (1/|G|) sum_g R_g Q R_g^dagger.

Thus equivalent-block averaging precedes orbital averaging in the adjoint;
the orientation of the orbital conjugations is reversed. The old diagnostic
symmetrized a moment before the inverse-square-root derivative. That is
equivalent only under additional symmetry/commutation assumptions.

BuildNormalizationAdjoint now runs first. SymmetrizeNormalizationAdjoint
then applies this exact reverse chain to Q. The routine respects the existing
conjugated storage convention Qbar=conjg(Q). It is enabled by default:

  EDMFT_STRESS_NORM_SYMMETRY=APPLY   default, correct chain
  EDMFT_STRESS_NORM_SYMMETRY=DIAG    print correction but omit it
  EDMFT_STRESS_NORM_SYMMETRY=OFF     old response, diagnostic only

The forward Cartesian/orbital derivative transforms jointly. The existing
final stress symmetry projection is still required for Cartesian indices;
the hydrostatic contraction is already invariant. Individual unsymmetrized
intermediate Cartesian components must not be interpreted as final stresses.

2. Why equivalent atoms exposed the shear inconsistency
======================================================
Equivalent atoms give tensor contributions related by rotation. If R maps
atom a to atom b, their full symmetry-related tensor contributions obey

  T_b = R T_a R^T,

with the k point, band gauge, and orbital representation also transformed
before making a state-resolved comparison. They need not have identical
components in fixed global Cartesian coordinates. Hydrostatic contraction is
invariant; a shear contraction generally is not. For the Fe operation
R=diag(1,-1,-1), xx,yy,zz,yz are even while xz and xy are odd.

Notation for the actual projector builder: B is the reciprocal Cartesian
matrix, r is the fractional equivalent-atom rotation (rotij), and
R=B r B^(-1). C is the stored internal correlated rotation (crotloc).
The physical local-axis map in global Cartesian coordinates is L=C R.
For global reciprocal vector K, the spherical harmonics use q=L K.

We use fixed physical Cartesian local axes, fixed fractional k/G labels,
and strain generator E_nu. Then

  K(t)=(1+t E_nu)^(-T) K,
  dK/dt=-E_nu K,
  dq/dt=-L E_nu K=-C R E_nu K.

For diagonal channels E_aa=1. For a stored shear channel ab, E_ab=E_ba=1/2;
the resulting derivative equals one symmetric tensor component, not twice it.

The old angular expression used -C E_nu R K. That expression assumes the
same numerical strain components in a different frame. It disagrees when
[R,E_nu] is nonzero. Other pieces already used shared Bessels differentiated
with global K, so the projector mixed two incompatible strain conventions.
The failing Fe channels were exactly xz and xy.

The repair uses global K and complete axes L consistently in the explicit
projector angular, matching/radial, and interstitial derivatives. The LO
builder has an explicit global_strain option for this path. Its default
retains the representative-atom convention used by the DFT G2 path, whose
tensor is rotated after contraction.

The finite-difference helper also required a correction. At a strain that
breaks the reference symmetry, B(t) r B(t)^(-1) is no longer an orthogonal
operation. Keeping the numerical C fixed would change physical axes L.
The helper now sets C(t)=L(0) R(t)^(-1), holding L fixed, and restores C
afterwards. This is a local basis-derivative test, not an assumption that the
strained crystal retains the old space group. Fractional phase factors are
unchanged under co-moving atoms and fixed fractional k/G labels.

This derivative test now succeeds independently for both atoms. A complete
relaxed shear calculation and a full k-star atom-resolved covariance test
have not been performed in this follow-up.

4. Can the density/potential response be computed affordably?
============================================================
Yes for the present frozen-effective-self-energy diagnostic, but it requires
another self-consistent response problem. It is not supplied by one bare
projector differentiation. No new impurity solve is needed. Here effective
self-energy means Sigma_tilde=Sigma_imp-V_DC held fixed; if V_DC is allowed
to change, its density derivative must also be included.

The subtlety is that a finite KS-band projector is a physical function of
the potential: Phi_W[V]=Pi_W[V] phi X[V], not merely a change of coordinates
in a fixed complete Hilbert space. Ordinary charge self-consistency n=n_G
therefore need not make the implemented finite-window functional stationary
against every V variation. In the fixed-basis convention of the preceding
audit its residual is

  delta_V Gamma = Re sum_k Tr[(R_k + R_k^N) delta V_k].

R_k is the Hermitian band response matrix from the fixed-X complementary
window derivative, and R_k^N is the additional normalization-window matrix.
The symbols R_k here are not the Cartesian symmetry rotation R above.
These matrices and their weights are defined in Sections 5 and 7 of the
preceding audit PDF. This residual was demonstrated in an independent finite
model; its magnitude in these materials has not yet been measured.

For reference, G=[(i omega+mu) O-H-U Sigma_tilde U^dagger]^(-1) gives

  delta G = G [delta H + delta(U Sigma_tilde U^dagger)
                -(i omega+mu) delta O - delta mu O] G.

The explicit geometry response supplies a source b_nu for the density.
With chi_fr the density response to induced Hxc potential at frozen
Sigma_tilde, and K_Hxc the derivative of Hxc potential with respect to n,

  n_nu = b_nu + chi_fr V_Hxc,nu,
  V_Hxc,nu = K_Hxc n_nu,
  (1-chi_fr K_Hxc) n_nu = b_nu.

n_nu=partial_nu n. K_Hxc contains Coulomb and XC response (including gradient
terms for GGA). chi_fr must include the moving finite-band window and its
normalization, rather than only the bare fixed-projector G delta V G term.
Chemical-potential response is determined by the chosen fixed-N constraint,
or set to zero for the separately specified fixed-mu insulating protocol.

This equation can be solved iteratively without forming a huge inverse or
storing a full real-space susceptibility. Central eigenvectors, Green
functions or their spectral representations, and static matrix elements can
be reused. Stress kernels need be contracted after response convergence,
not recomputed at every charge-response iteration. General stress may need
six directions; the present isotropic comparison needs one direction.

An analytic implementation has not been built or timed. A measured cost
reference is available: each recent SrVO3 +/-0.0005 frozen-Sigma endpoint
needed 20 charge iterations and approximately 195 seconds (3.3 minutes),
with MPI4, no impurity solve, and stress disabled during convergence. Those
two jobs ran concurrently. This supports feasibility on the laptop but is
not a timing prediction for a new analytic solver or for FeSe.

Practical next diagnostic, before implementing that solver:
  1. Use the existing converged +/- strain densities/potentials.
  2. Map them to common central coordinates and basis conventions, with the
     volume Jacobian and muffin-tin/interstitial pieces handled consistently.
  3. Isolate the induced Hxc variation from explicit ionic/geometric changes
     already counted in the current H_nu kernels. A naive subtraction of two
     total potential files can double count those explicit terms. One option
     is central-geometry lapw0 evaluation of consistently mapped densities.
  4. Contract the induced matrix derivative with R_k+R_k^N. Independently
     test stationarity at one geometry using a small charge-neutral potential
     perturbation and the frozen-Sigma tangent functional, plus a DFT control.
  5. Only after those tests identify a nonduplicated missing path derivative
     should it be added to a reported stress or implemented analytically.

Paired radial derivatives of H, O, and P remain another open issue. Adding
only the radial projector derivative would be inconsistent. Stored-band
complement convergence and window-boundary degeneracy remain separate
limitations. Neither the normalization nor frame fix proves that the
remaining GPa error is caused by potential response.
