CROSS-MATERIAL POTENTIAL-OPERATOR TEST AND ONE-LAPW0 DENSITY ESTIMATES
7 November 2026

Scope and authorization
=======================
No production source or executable was changed. All new runs are isolated
under /private/tmp/projector_cross_material_20261107. Original material
inputs and calculation outputs were not rewritten. Tests use existing
converged endpoint potentials; there was no new impurity or charge SCF run.
The user explicitly asked to test FeSe/MnO and whether one LAPW0 evaluation
on a simple strained density can estimate the missing potential derivative.

MT-only is an approximation: the omitted interstitial term is about 0.09 GPa

==============================

Minimum window/complement separation 0.004882997604 Ry, no skipped pairs.
Central dual-basis metric error <9.4e-13; central projected Hamiltonian

Independent spectral-projector endpoint derivatives in the extracted

-0.108113419491 Ry at h=5e-6, compared with direct response contraction
-0.108113415698 Ry. This is a projector chain-rule check with central
functional gradients, not a new fully relaxed Klein finite difference.

MnO: RELA, RKMAX7, 72 irreducible k points, 23 stored bands, W=[6,16].
Central metric error <2.1e-13; central H reconstruction <1.3e-12 Ry.
The zero window gap is retained as a failure of the smooth-window criterion.

Independent Fourier reconstruction reproduces central warped VNS coefficients
within 3.2e-14 Ry in FeSe and 4.2e-14 Ry in MnO. The nonsymmorphic FeSe
symmetry requires the correct transpose and translation phases; omitting
that transpose gives a large 0.1705 Ry reconstruction error and is rejected.
FCC MnO requires the primitive-cell volume while using the conventional
integer reciprocal labels, as in LAPW0's REAN0/SETFFT routines.

The same frozen-radial representation and stored-band complement limitation
as the preceding SrVO3 test apply. The complete central eigensystem is used
only to reconstruct an exact dual basis for potential matrix extraction;
it does not enlarge the DMFT response complement beyond the saved bands.
Only isotropic stress has been tested, not the full anisotropic tensor.

One additional LAPW0: what it can compute
=========================================
Yes: given a trial strained density, ONE extra LAPW0 call computes its new
ionic, Hartree, and XC potential. The central potential already exists.
No LAPW1, dmft2, mixer, impurity solve, or charge convergence is logically
needed just to produce that trial potential. Our extra LAPW1 runs are
measurement tools for central band matrix elements, not a requirement of
the proposed production algorithm once direct potential integrals exist.

For F=(1+eta)I=lambda I, J=det(F)=lambda^3, an affine-density ansatz is

 n_eta(F r) = n_0(r)/J,
 n_eta^a(r) = lambda^-3 n_0^a(r/lambda) in atom-centered physical radius,
 (partial_eta n_eta^a)_r = -3 n_0^a-r partial_r n_0^a.

This is a model for the response, not a consequence of stationarity or a
solution of the screened electronic response equation.

Inside WIEN's CLMSUM file the radial channel stores c_lm proportional to
r^2 n_lm, with the extra constant normalization for l=m=0. Therefore

 c_lm,eta(r) = lambda^-1 c_lm,0(r/lambda).

The factor is lambda^-1, NOT lambda^-3, for that stored radial quantity.
Cubic interpolation in log(r) was used. The interstitial density Fourier
star coefficients are scaled by lambda^-3; the integer G labels are held
fixed as the reciprocal cell changes. All lattice lengths and atomic
centers are strained; physical RMT values remain fixed. The total density,
not only its spherical MT part, is passed to LAPW0.

A more physical first guess keeps tightly bound core charge atom-centered:

 n_eta^a(r) = n_core,0^a(r)
              +lambda^-3 n_val,0^a(r/lambda).

CORE and CLMSUM spherical radial channels use compatible 4*pi*r^2 density
normalization in these files, so the core can be subtracted and restored
before/after transport. A general implementation should handle any core
leakage into the interstitial consistently; its boundary density is small
here, and the resulting total-charge check is explicitly retained below.

After running LAPW0 on the guessed density in the strained structure,

 (partial_eta V_MT)_r ~= [V_MT,eta(r)-V_MT,0(r)]/eta,
 Vop_MT ~= (partial_eta V_MT)_r + r partial_r V_MT,0,
 Vop_I  ~= [V_eta(F r)-V_0(r)]/eta in the central interstitial.

For the last expression use the unwarped potential output (R2V), or remove
the moving interstitial-step contribution from VNS. Do not add a second
coordinate derivative if the potential has already been pulled back to
material coordinates. Form its off-diagonal matrix elements on the central
KS states and contract with R+R^N.

How to improve the approximation without an impurity solve
=========================================================
LAPW0 faithfully evaluates V_KS[n_trial], but cannot infer how the electrons
relax. For a more accurate derivative one needs a better density direction:
  - frozen-Sigma charge linear response using central Green functions and
    the same moving window/normalization;
  - or a small number of frozen-Sigma charge updates on the trial structure,
    checking convergence of the contracted correction rather than assuming
    that one update is sufficient.
The familiar equation schematically reads

 delta n = b_eta + chi_frozenSigma delta V_Hxc,
 delta V_Hxc = f_Hxc delta n,
 (1-chi_frozenSigma f_Hxc) delta n = b_eta.

The geometric ionic/Coulomb/Jacobian source must be included consistently.
There is no general reason the affine density should solve this equation.
A full new impurity solution is not required for the agreed frozen-Sigma
tangent-functional validation, but a fully self-consistent DMFT response
problem is a different calculation.

Implementation conclusion
=========================
SrVO3 and FeSe support the missing potential-operator contraction derived
from the normalized finite-window projector. MnO does not validate it yet
and its large apparent discrepancy is unexplained. The formula should be
judged from the chain rule and controlled tests, not fitted to a pressure.
A default-off production diagnostic is a reasonable next implementation
step, with source-change permission requested first. Replacing its physical
potential response by a uniform-density guess is not accurate enough based
on the SrVO3 measurements above. No approximation or correction was enabled
by default during these tests.
