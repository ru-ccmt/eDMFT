WHAT IS NOW CONNECTED

For a strain parameter eta_nu, the installed charge engine supplies

  (I-J) q_nu = s_nu,
  V'_nu = v_explicit,nu + K q_nu,
  b_nu = b_geometry,nu + B V'_nu.

q is the density response in the central material coordinates. s is the
complete explicit normalized charge-map source, including spatial geometry,
core, radial changes and both native normalization/transport operations.
K is the prepared Hartree/XC density-to-potential operation. B maps a native
potential to joint H/O/raw-projector perturbations. b_geometry contains the
remaining explicit matrix/overlap/projector strain at fixed potential files.
J, K and B need not be symmetric; their tested transposes are used explicitly.

The new helper requires BOTH explicit band geometry and explicit potential.
Omitting either is an error. It supplies the following selected scalar readout:

  D_nu = g_b^T b_geometry,nu + g_V^T v_explicit,nu + c^T q_nu,
  g_V = B^T g_b,        c = K^T g_V.

One adjoint for this scalar gives

  (I-J)^T lambda = c,
  D_nu = g_b^T b_geometry,nu + g_V^T v_explicit,nu + lambda^T s_nu.

The same lambda serves all strain directions for ONE scalar functional. The
projector and lattice readouts are different observables and generally need
different adjoints. Their two solves here are diagnostics, not a requirement
for a future complete functional: its combined gradient needs one adjoint.
Six-column synthetic tests pass; six physical general strain sources are NOT
supplied by this checkpoint. The material tests use hydrostatic strain only.

THE SCALAR THAT IS DIFFERENTIATED

Let epsilon_k be the diagonal central KS energies, mu the central chemical
potential, U_k the normalized window projector, and Sigma_n the effective
local self-energy including the chosen double-counting subtraction. Define

  h_k = epsilon_k - mu I,
  h_inf,k = h_k + U_k Sigma_inf U_k^dagger,
  A_n,k = i omega_n I - h_k - U_k Sigma_n U_k^dagger.

The regularized lattice grand-potential scalar used in the tests is

  L = 2 sum_k w_k [ -T Tr log(1 + exp(-h_inf,k/T))
      + 2T sum_n W_n Re{log det(i omega_n I-h_inf,k)-log det A_n,k} ].

T and all energies are in Ry; W_n are the EXPORTED compressed Matsubara
quadrature weights. The first factor 2 is nonmagnetic spin degeneracy; the
second factor 2 combines positive and negative frequencies. rho and all
functional covectors already contain these weights and w_k. They must not
be inserted again. Every test uses the same exported quadrature and static
subtraction; this is not an independent assessment of the omitted tail error.

L includes the noninteracting valence contribution. It is NOT the complete
Klein functional: Hartree/XC double counting, ionic energy, core, and the
consistent impurity/DC tangent bookkeeping remain to be assembled. In a
frozen-local-Sigma check, changing U still changes its lattice embedding.

JOINT DERIVATIVE, INCLUDING THE NORMALIZED PROJECTOR

In the central orthonormal KS gauge, a generalized-basis perturbation gives

  h_eff' = H' - (O' epsilon + epsilon O')/2,
  phi_eff' = phi' - O' phi/2,
  U = Pi_W phi X,
  U' = Pi_W' phi X + Pi_W phi_eff' X + Pi_W phi X'.

The Pi_W and X derivatives are evaluated by the installed common electronic
response, with the actual overlap symmetrization and normalization. X' contains
both raw-overlap and window-motion contributions. The empty complementary
finite LAPW space contributes through the retained/complement shifted solves;
it is not dropped merely because it is outside the correlated band window.

The scalar derivative is

  L' = Re Tr(rho h_eff') + Re Tr(g_U^dagger U').

The second term, after the full window/normalization/metric pullback, is the
previously validated projector covector g_projector. The new direct covector
is especially simple in joint RR/QR/raw coordinates:

  g_direct,H_RR = rho,
  g_direct,O_RR = -(rho epsilon + epsilon rho)/2,
  g_direct,phi_R = g_direct,H_QR = g_direct,O_QR = 0,
  g_lattice = g_projector + g_direct.

R denotes retained KS states; Q completes the finite native LAPW space.
The direct Q blocks vanish under the prepared thermally empty/gapped Q policy.
The projector Q blocks need not vanish. Hermitian cross blocks use the real
pairing 2 Re Tr(g_QR^dagger b_QR). The installed packed coordinates include
this factor isometrically; the raw-projector complex pairing has no factor 2.

WHY NO ADDITIONAL ISOLATED M_SIGMA O' IS INSERTED

The production covariant formulation uses rho_E and contains the familiar
combination Re Tr(rho H' - rho_E O'). It can be rewritten in the symmetric
orthonormal gauge above. In that gauge the direct overlap weight is
{rho,epsilon}/2, while the self-energy-related metric dependence is carried
by the derivative of U. Appending another isolated self-energy overlap moment
to this complete orthonormal-gauge expression would generally count that
dependence twice. The two arrangements must be compared as complete sums.

This is a bookkeeping requirement, not a claim that a particular production
term is erroneous or should be deleted. Reconciliation with the actual
Fortran rho_E, Matsubara tail and projector baseline remains an explicit task.

A strong test is a pure infinitesimal change of basis S=I+eta A:

  H' = A^dagger epsilon + epsilon A,
  O' = A^dagger + A,
  phi' = A^dagger phi.

L'=0 for this coordinate change. The direct and projector contributions are
individually nonzero and cancel in the new tests. This also explains why
testing or adding an isolated overlap or projector term can be misleading.

NATIVE FINITE-DIFFERENCE CHECK AND CHEMICAL-POTENTIAL POLICY

The nonlinear oracle reads native eigenvalues and normalized projectors from
the independently run strained snapshots. It evaluates the same scalar using
an independent orbital-space determinant identity and checked LU factors.
Its invariance under KS band rotations avoids phase matching. No derivative
matrix is used to construct this oracle. Band count, window, k weights,
self-energy, temperature and quadrature are checked against the center.

At strictly fixed mu, SrVO3 has appreciable finite-step curvature from its
occupations. This caused an initially poor two-step extrapolation. The saved
native endpoint runs already supplied independently solved fixed-N values of
mu. They permit a smoother, equally valid first-derivative comparison:

  F_eta = L_eta(mu_eta) + (mu_eta - mu_0) N_0,
  dF/deta|_0 = partial_eta L|_(mu_0),

because partial_mu L=-N_0 at the central state. Here N_0 is the reference
VALENCE band number for this scalar, not the integer total charge including
core, and not the post-normalized total spatial density. The shifted constant
mu_0 N_0 is immaterial. Sigma(i omega_n) remains fixed in the supplied local
convention, as in the reference response. Both fixed-mu and fixed-N paths have
the same first derivative; their finite-step errors differ. Raw grand
potentials at different mu must NOT be compared without the Legendre term.

Two strain sizes were used on all tested meshes, with a third available on
the three-k fixtures. Results and Richardson limits are recorded below and
in scalar_refinement.json. The remaining smallest-step differences include
native input rounding and subtraction of large scalar values.

FORWARD/ADJOINT ERROR ACCOUNTING

For approximate q and lambda let r_f=s-(I-J)q and r_a=c-(I-J)^T lambda.
The difference between the two induced contractions is exactly

  c^T q - lambda^T s = r_a^T q - lambda^T r_f.

The reported absolute bound is ||r_a|| ||q|| + ||lambda|| ||r_f||. A small
GMRES callback residual alone is insufficient: the final true residual is
recomputed. Explicit geometry and potential terms are identical on both
sides. All readout values are in Ry per strain parameter; no isolated value
in this report is converted into a pressure correction.

1. The fixed-basis PHYSICAL-potential map P has now been implemented in
   lapw_physical_potential.py using the same discrete H assembly. It has a
   tested transpose and scalar contraction, with three-k material checks in
   SrVO3 and FeSe. Its scalar covector is P^T rho. In the
   ideal stationary rearrangement the ordinary physical-potential derivative
   cancels the corresponding density/potential term outside L. The remaining
   valence potential gradient is B^T g_lattice - P^T rho. This residual can
   contain both radial Pulay and normalized-projector response.

   Implemented code route: PreparedSphericalBand.action(...,
   difference_dot=delta(r V)) with all radial/matching tangents set to zero
   supplies the spherical fixed-basis physical variation; its pullback returns
   difference_bar. NativePotentialBands supplies explicit nonspherical and
   interstitial actions/pullbacks. NativeScreenedState.potential_selector
   supplies the common potential-file coordinate conversion. Independent
   spherical-integral, paired, nonlinear scalar and constant-potential tests
   pass. The constant-potential matrix equals identity in RR and zero in QR,
   and its density contraction reproduces the central valence particle number.

   We also record the potential-dependent MATRIX remainder
     g_remainder,V = B^T g_lattice - P^T rho
                  = g_projector,V + g_radial_basis,V.
   Its separation is now exact for these prepared maps. It has NOT yet been
   identified with the complete correction to the actual spatial Klein
   functional or the existing analytic stress. In particular this subtraction
   alone supplies neither core nor explicit geometry terms outside L.

2. Reconcile that matrix-potential contraction with the actual spatial density,
   core, Hartree/XC and charge normalization used in the Klein implementation.
   WIEN24 H/O uses CHARGE radial quadrature; dmft2's ATPARN/projector/density
   path uses its historical RINT13 quadrature. They are separate maps in the
   response engine. Do not silently replace one contraction by the other.
   The archived centers have nonzero charge fixed-point residuals, especially
   the deliberately small three-k fixtures. Stationarity cannot be assumed
   to remove their residual terms numerically.

   ADDITIONAL PAIRING AUDIT: the interstitial matrix and raw spatial-density
   contractions agree near roundoff. Their MT contractions initially differ.
   A controlled calculation at FIXED central coefficients separates two causes:
   the native nonspherical Hamiltonian uses LNSMAX=4, whereas the spatial
   density uses wavefunction channels through l=8; the radial banks also differ.
   Raising only the DIAGNOSTIC potential contraction to l<=8 removes nearly
   all of the nonspherical discrepancy. Using l<=8 AND the density's ATPARN
   radial bank makes that contraction agree with the spatial integral near
   roundoff. This identifies a concrete finite-cutoff cancellation issue, not
   a missing impurity vertex. It does not establish the pressure error caused
   by that issue, and no LNSMAX input, Hamiltonian or material run was changed.

   The prospective functional cancellation therefore needs explicit handling
   of this native angular truncation as well as radial quadrature. We must not
   silently identify P^T rho with an integral of the higher-l native density.
   Detailed numbers are in spatial_pairing.json and angular_cutoff.json. A
   physical comparison after raising LNSMAX and reconverging remains a future
   test; these same-coefficient diagnostics cannot replace it.

   The analogous spherical check compares Hamiltonian channels through l=10
   with density channels through l=8. Restricting the diagnostic contraction
   to l<=8 reduces that smaller residual too. The remaining FeSe spherical
   pairing error after using the density bank is recorded, not declared zero.
   Thus the proposed final gradient must include a separately verified
   matrix-minus-spatial pairing term in addition to projector and radial
   response, or use consistently converged cutoffs. The present calculation
   does not yet assemble the core/input-density/normalization counterparts.

PERFORMANCE FOLLOW-UP

A cProfile measurement of one prepared three-k FeSe J^T action takes 1.824 s
with profiling enabled. NativePotentialBands.pullback accounts for 1.255 s
(about 69%), and its spherical pullbacks for 1.071 s. Repeated rectangular
right_multiply_columns and array validation are significant within that cost.
The cached Matsubara response is not the main cost in this particular profile.
Full-mesh FeSe transpose actions measured separately take about 47-50 s.
The small-mesh profile identifies routines to investigate, not a quantitative
full-mesh speedup prediction. FeSe_transpose_profile.txt contains the evidence.

After the functional is reconciled, optimize/reuse the repeated spherical
RR/QR contractions, move redundant validation to the prepared-map boundary
where safe, and parallelize the independent k/site reductions. Preserve all
paired, gauge and native scalar checks. The final combined correction should
require ONE adjoint for all six strains; the two adjoints per case here test
two separate diagnostic observables. No production acceleration is claimed.
