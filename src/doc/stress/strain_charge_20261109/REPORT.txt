WHICH DERIVATIVE IS s_nu?

Let eta be the scalar strain amplitude and e the strain direction. For uniform
linear lattice scaling, e=I and a_eta=(1+eta)a. RMT, physical radial grids,
radial linearization energies, local axes and local Sigma/DC are fixed.
The finite LAPW G/LO labels and DMFT band indices are fixed as well.

n0 is the input central charge in material coordinates. T_eta pushes it to
the strained native coordinates. In the existing scalar transport convention,
for c(r)=r^2 times a density harmonic and log-radius x=log(r),
  T_eta c = (1+eta)^(-1) c(x-log(1+eta)),
  T_eta rho_G = (1+eta)^(-3) rho_G.
Radial interpolation uses the existing cubic spline, including at MT edges.
Therefore tau_n = partial_eta T_eta n at eta=0 is
  (tau_n)_MT = -c - dc/dx,    (tau_n)_PW = -3 rho_G.          (1)
The derivative of inverse transport has the opposite sign.

V_eta[T_eta n] is the LAPW0 potential map. N_eta denotes the physical native
MIXER CHARGE/SETN/NORMAL normalization of the raw valence+core OUTPUT, before
mixing. It is not a mixer iteration. The independent native reference uses
the following material-coordinate charge map:

 F_eta(n)=N_0 { T_eta^(-1) N_eta [ n_val(eta,V_eta[T_eta n],Sigma)
                                  + n_core(eta,V_eta[T_eta n]) ] }.       (2)

The N_0 outside braces is a SECOND normalization after inverse transport.
It is already part of the native reference used for the screened tests; it
cannot be omitted from the analytic source while comparing to that reference.
At eta=0 it leaves the normalized output unchanged.

Define J=partial_n F_0 at n0 and s_nu=partial_eta F_eta(n0) at fixed n0.
The induced material-charge derivative solves
  (I-J) q_nu = s_nu.                                        (3)
Away from a converged central state this is the local tangent equation; it
is not automatically the derivative of a converged material calculation.
The recorded central fixed-point residuals must be checked before making a
physical stress comparison. No impurity vertex is included: Sigma/DC is fixed.

ASSEMBLING THE RAW OUTPUT DERIVATIVE

Use the following maps, all prepared at the same central state:
  K: charge -> native Hartree/XC potential coefficients;
  B: potential -> covariant H/O and RAW projector changes;
  E: joint H/O/raw input -> weighted band density-matrix change;
  R: weighted band density matrix -> spatial valence density;
  D_V: explicit radial/matching density response to spherical potential;
  C_V: core-density response to potential.
Band density matrices already include spin and k weights. They must not be
weighted again during spatial reconstruction. E handles metric, window and
inverse-square-root projector normalization exactly once.

The explicit potential input is
  s_V = K tau_n0 + (partial_eta V_eta)_fixed native density,  (4)
including ionic Coulomb, pseudocharge and boundary geometry, XC gradient/
Hessian scaling, and the moving interstitial potential mask.
The geometric joint source b_geometry holds potential FILE coefficients
fixed, as documented in band_geometry_20261108. Thus
  b_exp = b_geometry + B s_V,
  delta rho_exp = E b_exp.                                 (5)

At fixed band density, explicit spatial geometry contributes d_val,geometry.
In the MT region both augmentation legs are required:
  delta n_MT = D_spatial[Z' rho Z^dagger + Z rho Z'^dagger].  (6)
D_spatial denotes radial/angular reconstruction into native density harmonics.
Z' uses the same analytic angular/Bessel/matching/LO derivative as the band
helper, but with the actual dmft2 density radial quadrature and local frames.
Equivalent sites are averaged with the native 1/multiplicity factor.

In fractional-coordinate interstitial Fourier space the fixed coefficient
continuation only changes through its 1/Omega normalization:
  (d_val,geometry)_PW = -tr(e) n_val,PW.                     (7)
clmval is an UNMASKED continuation; adding the moving sphere-exclusion mask
here would count it twice. That mask enters charge integration below.

There is no explicit core radial-geometry change at fixed RMT/radial grid and
fixed potential. Its potential response is C_V s_V. Changes of the core
charge's material-coordinate representation enter inverse transport later.
The total raw-output source is therefore
  d_raw = R E(b_geometry+B s_V) + D_V s_V + C_V s_V
          + d_val,geometry.                               (8)

NATIVE NORMALIZATION AND INVERSE TRANSPORT

For raw central output y0, let M=m.y0 be MT charge, I=i.y0 IR charge,
Qtarget fixed target charge, alpha=(Qtarget-M)/I, and p0=y0 with MT entries
zeroed. The normalization derivative at y0 is the already installed
  N_raw x = diag(1_MT,alpha_PW)x - p0 (m+alpha i).x/I.        (9)
It must be prepared at raw OUTPUT y0, not input n0 or normalized output.

At fixed RMT the MT integration covector m is constant. For IR Fourier
star sums, the integrated mask is
  Theta_G=Omega delta_G0-sum_a 4 pi R_a^3 f(|G|R_a) phase_a,
  f(x)=j1(x)/x, f(0)=1/3, phase_a=exp(-i G.R_a).
For e symmetric and gamma_G=(G.e.G)/|G|^2,
  Theta_G'=tr(e)Omega delta_G0
           +sum_a 4 pi R_a^3 gamma_G [j0(x)-3f(x)] phase_a. (10)
This is an integrated mask, unlike the normalized finite-FFT potential mask.
Native inversion-at-origin handling and real-coordinate complex pairing are
preserved. Since density entries are star SUMS, no star multiplicity is added.
Let I_geometry=i'.y0. The explicit normalized-coordinate source is
  n_geometry=-p0 alpha I_geometry/I.                        (11)

Let ybar0=N_0(y0) be the central normalized OUTPUT. The complete source is
  s_nu = N_post [N_raw d_raw + n_geometry - tau_ybar0],       (12)
where N_post is the derivative of central normalization at ybar0 (alpha=1).
The last term is inverse transport of ybar0, not of n0. At a nonzero central
fixed-point residual these charges differ. The existing J needs no extra
operation: its output is charge-conserving and N_post acts as identity on
that tangent range. The explicit geometry source need not lie in that range
until all terms in (12) have been assembled.

FESE CELL ROUNDING AND THE DIAGONAL FIELD EXTENSION

Six-decimal native cell lengths give a slightly anisotropic finite-difference
direction in FeSe: diag(0.999874011760,0.999874011760,1.000251976481).
The new field helper differentiates the SAME native Coulomb/XC map for that
actual symmetry-preserving diagonal direction. It includes YKAV angular
motion, reciprocal-length changes and volume changes separately:
  q'=-e q; (8*pi/q^2)'=2 gamma_q (8*pi/q^2);
  grad n'=-e grad n; Hessian n'=-e Hessian n-Hessian n e.
Input/output density transport still uses the scalar mean strain, as in the
native reference. The diagonal extension is not general shear support or
a physical anisotropic density-transport implementation.
For e=I it agrees with the installed hydrostatic potential helper to roundoff.
Independent nonlinear operator rebuilds test its Coulomb, LDA/PBE and angular
derivatives, not just equality to the previous helper.

the additional smaller-step check. The interstitial subset retains errors of
order 1e-6 to a few 1e-6. That smaller residual has not been assigned to a
specific remaining discretization/implementation effect and is not hidden by
the much larger radial-coordinate norm. No claim of roundoff accuracy is made.

The screened comparisons reuse the checked J and solve (3), starting from the
old numerical-source solution to avoid unnecessary iterations. Final TRUE
residuals are independently evaluated. The analytic source itself needs no
native LAPW0/LAPW1/DMFT2 launches, finite differences or nonlinear SCF loop.
The construction/solve timings below are preparation and warm-start timings,
not an end-to-end production relaxation benchmark.
