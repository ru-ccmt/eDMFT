Physical conventions
--------------------
Here eta strains all three lengths by (1+eta). Mean tensile stress is
  sigma_bar = [dK/deta]/(3 Omega).
Compression-positive thermodynamic pressure is -sigma_bar. Every GPa number
below uses this convention. A separate window contribution is NOT an additive
correction to an already assembled total stress.

1. Fixed-band window: what was measured
--------------------------------------
The default projector/window scheme is unchanged. In the staged diagnostic,
only inverse energy denominators with |epsilon_in-epsilon_out| <= cutoff are
replaced by zero. Central eigenvectors, band membership, projectors, X, k-point
weights and densities remain unchanged. The regular pairs at an affected k
point remain included. Diagnostic contexts cannot publish a total stress.
Default strict validation still rejects an unresolved window degeneracy.
The existing native DMFT2 code already skips denominators <=1e-10 Ry in its
window response; this is not a newly proposed physical regularization.

MnO: upper cut 16/17 is degenerate at k points 1--8 (nine omitted pairs).
MnTe: upper cut 51/52 is degenerate at k points 4,97--100.

Window-plus-normalization component contracted against endpoint-derived H/O:
  MnO: +34.0899437023 GPa, unchanged for cutoffs 1e-12 through 1e-3 Ry.
  MnTe: raising cutoff to 1e-4 Ry changes the spin-averaged component by
        about +7.49e-7 GPa; individual spin changes largely cancel.

Dropping ALL window-motion terms at affected k points instead changes that
component by -8.02799737 GPa (MnO) and +0.002788667 GPa (MnTe).
Do not interpret these as changes of the full screened total stress. In
particular, discarding whole k points is not justified by these results.

Finite random rotations within the cut-degenerate cluster, recomputing X,
changed the frozen-Sigma lattice scalar by at most 2.2355e-7 Ry for MnO and
about 1.6254e-8 Ry for spin-averaged MnTe in the sampled tests. Dividing these
finite gauge changes by h*3*Omega (h=.001) gives scales .00740 and .000118 GPa.
These are neither rigorous bounds nor measured stress derivatives. At an exact
cut degeneracy the unregularized fixed-subspace derivative is not unique;
a cutoff plateau alone does not establish differentiability.

Complete screened MnO diagnostic (fixed mu, actual INM=NO policy):
  cutoff 1e-10 Ry: sigma_bar = -72.3141607458 GPa
  cutoff 1e-4 Ry:  sigma_bar = -72.3141607458 GPa (unchanged).
  dK/deta = -2.18435048600 Ry
  explicit = -.707104887509 Ry; induced = -1.47724559849 Ry.
The saved endpoint reference is -72.3517105902 GPa, a difference of .03755 GPa.

Complete screened MnTe diagnostic (shared fixed N, actual INM=YES policy):
  cutoff 1e-10 Ry: sigma_bar = -.668175369232 GPa
  cutoff 1e-4 Ry:  sigma_bar = -.668175086755 GPa
  cutoff sensitivity = +2.8248e-7 GPa.
The saved endpoint reference is -.667209300357 GPa, a difference of .000966 GPa.
One common adjoint serves both magnetic branches. The two complete runs took
503 and 478 seconds on four MPI ranks, including preparation; these overlapped

Important correction to the initial diagnostic: MnO uses INM=NO, but the
first harness incorrectly enabled charge normalization, producing +1.00584
GPa. That result and its apparent +73.3 GPa adjoint residual correction belong
to the WRONG charge map and are superseded. Matching the actual policy removes
the large analytic/numerical discrepancy without changing the user calculation.
The staged loader records INM and the screened-state adapter now rejects a
normalization-policy mismatch instead of silently differentiating another map.

These remain diagnostic hydrostatic results, not production-approved complete

MnTe's density residual exceeds the current publication threshold, and MnO's

2. MnO inputs and fixed-mu sensitivity
-------------------------------------
All three MnO IN1 reference energies now agree: .485779237133333 Ry.
The captured EF.dat values are center 6.6083422596215815 eV and endpoints
6.609509262416999 eV (difference 1.167 meV). Do not confuse IN1's radial-search
reference energy with the interacting chemical potential in EF.dat.

At fixed saved density/potential and frozen Sigma, evaluating the endpoint
lattice scalars with a common mu shifted by +/-1 meV changes the numerical
stress estimate by approximately -/+ .0186424 GPa; +/-10 meV gives
-/+ .18642 GPa. This is a sensitivity test, not a relaxed correction or a
thermodynamic compressibility calculation. At the common reference mu,
valence N was 21.00070181 (center), 20.99271755 (minus), 21.00804087 (plus).
The charge error is small but its strain dependence is measurable.

For K_mu=Omega_Sigma+mu*N_target, partial_mu K_mu=N_target-N. If mu changes
along a path, its extra contribution is (N_target-N)*dmu/deta. At strictly
fixed mu this path term vanishes. The code already includes its mu*N and
charge bookkeeping; appending an independent mu*dN term would double count.
No chemical-potential or particle-number policy was changed.

3. Geometry and native convention fixes in the staged patch
----------------------------------------------------------
New lapw_lattice.py reuses wlatgen.Latgen and keeps BR1 (stored G labels) and
BR2 (primitive reciprocal basis) distinct. It provides Cartesian reciprocal
momenta, primitive volume, fractional-to-Cartesian rotations and centering
translations for P/S/F/B/H/R/CXY/CXZ/CYZ. Centered FFT masks use the allowed
reflection projector; conventional volume is not substituted for primitive
volume. Tests exercise arbitrary symmetric deformations of these maps.

Additional native conventions needed by these cases:
* Hexagonal PBE uses WIEN's 21-point finite-difference stencil, versus 27 points
  for the other tested FFT geometry, with the full Cartesian cell metric.
* Cubic harmonic input compression, XC output expansion and the C^T C energy
  pairing are distinct maps. The nonspherical Hamiltonian uses the matching
  native CBCOMB convention as well.
* DMFT2 puts the identity symmetry first before ROTDEF. Reconstructing the
  same charge symmetry is insufficient: the raw local projector gauge must
  also match that actual ordering.
* Native ROTDEF repairs finite-precision symmetry-equivalent positions.
  Reproducing this in memory removes errors from STRUCT fractions such as 1/3;
  no STRUCT file is edited.

Empty stored-band degeneracy is separate from a DMFT-window degeneracy.
Complement solves omit only columns independently proven thermally empty and
outside the DMFT window, with charge and slope bounds below 1e-12. Occupied or
DMFT-window columns cannot be disabled. The stored space itself is unchanged.

Coverage is not yet universal production stress support. Native F-cell MnO
and H-cell MnTe were tested. B/R/centered monoclinic cells have geometry tests,
not full material stress comparisons. General shear publication is still
rejected when symmetry permits shear; it must not silently return zero.

4. Magnetic response and chemical-potential workflow
---------------------------------------------------
User clarification verified in all three MnTe params.dat files:
  recomputeEF=1; MnTe uses adjusting mu, NOT a fixed-mu insulator policy.
Changing EF.dat across strained structures is expected here. The initial
fixed-mu MnTe stress job was stopped and replaced by a fixed-N job.

The existing run_dmft.py workflow is preserved:
* Ordinary compensated magnet: first dmft2 spin updates mu; second uses it.
* Ferro=True with adjusting mu: FermiF computes the common EF before dmft2;
  both calls then reuse it. The response must not infer fixed-mu physics just
  because the individual Ferro dmft2 inputs temporarily use recomputeEF=0.

The staged low-level response treats the two electronic branches separately,
with one common unpolarized KS potential, core response, charge normalization,
HXC kernel and screened adjoint. Native branch quantities contain a factor of
2, so physical spin sums are represented by (branch_up+branch_down)/2.
This is normalization bookkeeping, NOT an assumption of equal spin densities.

For an arbitrary magnetic state:
  delta N_up + delta N_down = 0,
not delta N_up=delta N_down and not separate conservation of each population.
The magnetization can respond. MnTe's hydrostatic compensated altermagnetic
state should additionally respect its spin-interchanging symmetry. The central
exports have physical valence N_up=30.9999999878 and N_down=31.0000068780:
a residual imbalance of -6.89e-6 electrons, with exactly the same exported mu.
A full strain-induced magnetization check remains to be done.

If E_mu is the independent fixed-mu block response, z=d(rho_coeff)/dmu,
b.x=delta N at fixed mu, and chi=dN/dmu, the shared constraint is
  E_N x = E_mu x - z (b.x)/chi.
The number covector includes Tr(rho O') as well as Tr(delta rho_coeff).
The transpose and MPI reductions use exactly the same rank-one term. Half
factors from native spin normalization cancel in delta mu. No impurity vertex
or spin-dependent DFT potential is introduced.

Automatic export/launcher and magnetic total-stress publication are not yet
extended by this patch. The prepared two-spin response is available for
controlled diagnostics; a guard prevents accidental production publication.

5. Validation and remaining discrepancies
-----------------------------------------
283 component tests passed, including native radial/core libraries, lattice
maps, cubic coordinates, hexagonal XC derivatives, magnetic shared-number and
transpose tests, window diagnostics, and empty-complement degeneracy tests.
A nonlinear two-endpoint common-mu test with unequal spin populations also
agrees with the analytic shared-number tangent; compensation is not assumed.
A first rerun omitted the native-library environment and failed to load two
libraries; the final run with the correct environment has no failures/skips.

Native agreement:
* MnO central Hamiltonian relative error 9.59e-13, overlap 4.41e-13.
* Native scalar C_DEN: MnO error -3.74e-9 Ry; MnTe -2.32e-9 Ry, at printed
  native precision. Independent neutral-density LAPW0 finite differences
  agree at roughly 1e-6 Ry derivative accuracy (limited by printed energies).
* Native valence density reconstruction: MnO 1.14e-12, MnTe spin average
  1.14e-10 relative error. Native core reconstruction below 2e-13.
* Complete charge-map transpose tests: MnO 2.9e-14; two-spin MnTe 1.9e-14.
  The latter native test used fixed mu; shared fixed-N also has unit tests.
* SrVO3 end-to-end MPI-4 regression: mean tensile stress 2.933955149924 GPa,
  versus previous 2.933955149891; difference <4e-11 GPa. Its previous numerical
  result is 2.933152904042 GPa. Timing here overlaps other jobs and is not a
  speed benchmark.

Independent reconstruction from fresh one-pass endpoint exports gives
  MnO numerical mean stress:  -72.3517105902 GPa
  MnTe numerical mean stress:  -0.6672093004 GPa.
These agree with the previous endpoint postprocessing. After matching the
response ensemble and INM policy, the analytic/numerical discrepancies are
small; neither whole-k deletion nor a change of projector scheme was needed.

Central fixed-point residual norms in native density coordinates (not electron
counts): MnO .00036333 (INM=NO); MnTe .00503141 (INM=YES). The current publication tolerance is
.001, so the MnTe central snapshot fails it. Endpoint residuals are recorded
in fixedpoint_probe.json. Passing native reconstruction checks does not prove
SCF convergence. Tighter fixed points and consistent central mu are needed to assign a final
precision to the remaining small analytic/numerical differences.
